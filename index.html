INDEPENDENT BRAND IDENTITY PROPOSAL

A crest for the next chapter.

A proposal to preserve Coventry City FC’s civic heritage while rebuilding its crest into a clearer, more scalable identity system for contemporary football culture.

Coventry City Football Club Identity Renewal Proposal Park Jaeyoung · Seoul 2026

01 / THE MOMENT

25 YEARS AWAY. ONE NEW BEGINNING.

Back where you belong.

The 2026/27 season marks Coventry City’s return to the Premier League after a 25-year absence. A sporting return of this scale is more than a change of division — it is a moment when the club is once again seen by a truly global audience.

A modern football identity now has to live everywhere at once. The crest becomes a shirt badge, a broadcast mark, a social avatar, an app icon, a stadium sign, a retail object and a supporter symbol. That shift creates a simple design question: can Coventry become clearer without becoming less Coventry?

This proposal sees the return to the top flight as a natural moment to review how the club’s existing visual heritage performs under greater visibility. The aim is not to create a fashionable new symbol for the sake of change. It is to make an already meaningful identity easier to recognise, easier to reproduce and easier to extend.

02 / WHY IT MATTERS TO THE CLUB

A PRACTICAL CASE FOR RENEWAL

This is not only a logo question. It is a club-wide consistency question.

For Coventry City FC, the value of a stronger crest system would be operational as much as visual. One mark has to be managed by football, retail, media, partnerships, stadium, community and external production teams — often across very different formats.

A clearer identity system can reduce the amount of interpretation required every time the crest is reproduced. It can give internal teams and external partners a more stable asset to work with, while giving supporters a mark that still feels rooted in the club they already know.

VISIBILITY

Ready for a larger stage

Top-flight exposure places the club mark in more broadcasts, feeds, fixtures, thumbnails and international contexts. A clearer silhouette helps the identity register quickly without asking the viewer to read every detail.

CONSISTENCY

One system for many teams

A more controlled badge structure gives club departments, suppliers and partners a consistent reference across kit, signage, digital content, print and physical production.

COMMERCIAL USE

More flexible as a brand asset

A crest that performs confidently in full colour, one colour, embroidery and small-scale use can support a broader range of merchandise and branded applications without losing its character.

SUPPORTER CONTINUITY

Change without disconnecting memory

The proposal deliberately keeps the symbols supporters recognise. The ambition is evolution with continuity: clearer presentation, not a break from the club’s visual history.

Promotion creates attention. A stronger identity system helps the club use that attention consistently.

03 / WHY PROPOSE CHANGE?

Heritage is powerful.
Complexity is not the same thing.

The existing Coventry City crest is built on a rich symbolic foundation. Its elephant, castle, eagle, phoenix, flame, football and club name carry history, pride and resilience.

The problem is not a lack of meaning. The challenge is how all of that meaning performs in contemporary brand environments. At smaller scales, detailed illustration and a broad colour range can lose clarity. Different production methods can also emphasise different parts of the crest, making the identity feel less controlled from one application to another.

The proposal therefore does not aim to remove heritage, but to reorganise it into a clearer hierarchy with stronger reproduction, recognition and consistency. The question is not how little can remain. The question is how clearly the important parts can be seen.

01

Small-scale legibility

Fine illustration becomes harder to read in score graphics, social profiles, mobile interfaces and other compact environments.

02

Production variability

Embroidery, patch production, print and signage each reproduce detail differently. A more controlled structure creates more predictable results.

03

Visual hierarchy

When every symbol, colour and line competes equally, the crest can lose a strong first impression. Hierarchy allows the eye to recognise the club before reading the details.

04

System consistency

A contemporary club identity needs to remain coherent across matchday, media, retail and physical space rather than relying on separate treatments for each context.

04 / KEEP THE MEMORY

Do not replace history.
Reframe it.

The renewal is based on inheritance rather than replacement. Each symbol remains because each one carries a different part of Coventry’s civic and football identity.

The redesign changes drawing, proportion, line weight and hierarchy — not the story itself. The objective is to make those inherited symbols easier to recognise and easier to use without stripping away the identity supporters already know.

05 / CREST RENEWAL

One circle.
A clearer order.

The renewed design transforms the illustrative heraldic composition into a structured circular football badge.

A bold navy outer ring creates a stable silhouette and integrates the club name into one clear lock-up. Inside, the elephant and three towers remain the primary heritage anchor, while the eagle, phoenix, football and flame are reorganised into a more controlled visual hierarchy.

The difference between before and after is therefore not the deletion of history, but the creation of order. The renewed badge is intended to feel familiar before it feels new.

06 / COLOR SYSTEM

Sky Blue is not decoration.
It is recognition.

The renewed palette clarifies which colours lead and which colours support.

Sky Blue becomes the emotional signature. Navy gives structure and authority. White creates clarity and breathing room. Red is reserved for selected symbolic accents so that it adds energy without competing with the core identity.

This hierarchy is intended to make the identity easier to manage across full-colour, one-colour and reversed applications without creating a different visual personality every time the production method changes.

07 / APPLICATION SYSTEM

Not just a badge.
A club-wide identity tool.

The renewed crest is intended as a practical brand asset, not a logo that exists only on a presentation board.

It needs to move naturally from the chest of a shirt to supporter flags, digital content, stadium graphics, vehicles, merchandise and printed materials while remaining unmistakably the same identity. The studies below test the proposal across scale, material and context.

Each application asks a different question of the badge. Can it remain legible in motion? Can it survive embroidery? Can it work from a distance? Can it become a retail object without losing its civic character? The proposal is designed as one answer across all of those conditions.

08 / THE PROPOSAL IN ONE SENTENCE

Keep what supporters recognise. Improve everything the system has to do around it.

This proposal is intentionally evolutionary. It does not ask Coventry City FC to exchange history for modernity. It asks whether the same history can be organised more effectively for the next period of the club’s growth.

A successful renewal would still feel unmistakably Coventry on first sight — but would give the club a clearer, more consistent and more adaptable asset for the environments in which football now operates.
